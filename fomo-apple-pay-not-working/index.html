FOMO Apple Pay checks

Four checks before you retry.

01

Are you on mobile?

FOMO's April 29, 2026 web announcement says Apple Pay and debit-card onramps are available through the mobile app. If Apple Pay is missing on desktop web, move to the official mobile app rather than assuming the service is broken.

02

Use the live Add Funds flow.

FOMO's February 8, 2026 Apple Pay answer directs users to open the app, go to add funds, choose Apple Pay and confirm with Face ID or Touch ID. The exact screens can change, so follow the current in-app flow.

03

Check limits and your bank.

In a public App Store developer response dated February 16, 2026, FOMO said Apple Pay can fail for reasons including deposit limits and banks blocking transactions. That is not an exhaustive list, but it is a better starting point than repeatedly retrying.

04

Separate funding from trading fees.

FOMO's January recap says its native fiat-to-crypto onramp is integrated through Coinbase. Third-party onramp costs or limits are separate from FOMO's trading-fee schedule and can change independently.

ALTERNATIVE

Use another supported funding route.

If Apple Pay is unavailable or declined, FOMO's published mobile flows also describe debit-card funding and crypto deposits. Use only methods available to you legitimately; do not try to bypass bank, location or eligibility restrictions.

SUPPORT

Still stuck?

For a persistent failure, especially if money appears pending or charged, stop retrying and contact the provider or FOMO support with the transaction details you can safely share.

Email FOMO support →
Do not treat a declined Apple Pay attempt as proof that FOMO is down. A decline can happen upstream at the bank or onramp layer. Likewise, do not assume a successful deposit means a trade is low-risk.
